# Game Guide — Bellwether

## The premise

You begin at age three in Bellwether. A little brass toy train has a zero engraved beneath it. Over a lifetime, ordinary decisions, relationships and discoveries gradually reveal that the town's old railway accident was connected to a machine called the Meridian Engine.

The Engine records echoes of lives that might have happened. The mystery is told through school records, radio broadcasts, impossible transactions, contradictory family memories and the abandoned station.

## Your first few minutes

1. Choose **Begin a New Life**.
2. Enter a name and deliberately select one of the six appearances.
3. Make a choice during **The Toy Train** opening event.
4. Explore Home Lane and Willow Park. Walk close to a place or person and press **E**.
5. Try activities to develop traits and skills.
6. Use **Move into the next chapter** to progress to age 7, where the school mystery begins.

## Main mystery

### The Fourth Desk
The class photograph and school register do not agree. Visit Classroom 3B, collect the clues and choose the student whose name was removed: Elin Wren.

### The Radio That Answers Tomorrow
As a teenager, investigate the radio club's strange signal. Return to Classroom 3B and decode the frequency using the notebook clue.

### The Shop That Sells Nothing
As an older character, inspect the market ledger and ask Jo about a receipt for a ticket that appears to have been purchased in the future.

### The House with Two Birthdays
Inspect the dates carved into the Vale House gate. Return to the house after finding more evidence to discover Ada's letter.

### Station Zero
Collect evidence from different parts of town. The station ticket counter eventually reveals the Meridian sequence puzzle. Solving it leads to a choice: shut the Engine down, preserve the echoes or enter one.

## Side stories

Optional stories include Kit's folded bird, Chalk Dust Constellation, The Window That Lights Up, Lost Property, Found Tomorrow, The Bellmaker's Debt, A Map with No North, The Friend Who Forgot and The Collector of Tomorrow. Some are only available after reaching a certain age or finding a related clue.

## Identity paths

Identity is not a random roll. Skills, relationships, personality and life history make opportunities possible; the player makes the final choice in the Identity Index.

- **Scholar:** study, observation and curiosity
- **Entrepreneur:** business skills and resources
- **Caregiver:** empathy and helping others
- **Artist:** creativity and openness
- **Adventurer:** exploration and risk tolerance
- **Master Criminal:** stealth, risk and hidden contacts
- **Eccentric Inventor:** technical skill and creativity
- **Underground Kingpin:** influence, business and the hidden network
- **Time Traveller:** Meridian evidence, technical skill and the Engine
- **Ultra-Rich King:** substantial resources and influence

Discovering an identity does not end the game. It adds a role activity and special interactions at relevant locations. The identity index persists across new lives.

## Notes on age progression

This prototype uses meaningful age-stage jumps (3 → 7 → 13 → 19 → 31+) rather than simulating every birthday. Activities advance the day, consume energy and change skills or relationships. Rest restores energy. This keeps the life loop playable without turning the prototype into a repetitive calendar simulator.
